## Question

Do the observable planning traces from the pooled `32`-game provider championship support the hypothesis that Gemini is more explicitly goal-directed than the other providers?

The claim here is narrow.

It is **not**:
- “Gemini sounds smarter”
- “Gemini has better hidden reasoning”

It is:
- Gemini may track the actual win objective more explicitly in its visible planning text
- Gemini may quantify distance to the `65%` target more often
- that persistent objective tracking may help inside a bounded live agent loop

## Data And Method

Dataset:
- pooled provider series: `/shared-game-results/experiment_series/frontier_strategic_provider_32_pooled`
- `32` games
- `946` saved turn summaries with observable plan text

Method:
- analyze only the saved observable `plan.text` from `turn_summary_turn_*.json`
- do **not** use hidden reasoning
- use a reproducible lexical coding pass from [scripts/analyze_provider_trace_patterns.py](../../../scripts/analyze_provider_trace_patterns.py)

Tracked planning features:
- `explicit_endgame_goal`
  - win-target language such as `win`, `win target`, `65%`, `need X more`
- `quantified_goal`
  - explicit numeric tracking such as `need 17 more`, `28`, `65%`
- `aggressive_action`
  - verbs like `clear`, `sweep`, `break`, `secure`, `finish`, `blitz`
- `contingency`
  - branching or fallback language such as `if`, `otherwise`, `backup`, `skip`
- `card_focus`
  - explicit card-oriented subgoals
- `continent_focus`
  - continent/bonus-oriented framing

Two normalizations are reported:
- share of plans containing a feature
- feature count per `100` words, to control partly for verbosity differences

## Headline Result

Gemini is the only provider in this pooled series that treats the win objective as a standing variable in the visible plan text.

Plan share with explicit endgame-goal language:
- `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`: `58.5%`
- `claude-opus-4-7`: `3.1%`
- `kimi-k2.6`: `1.4%`
- `gpt-5.1`: `0.4%`

Plan share with quantified goal language:
- `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`: `54.5%`
- `claude-opus-4-7`: `3.5%`
- `gpt-5.1`: `0.8%`
- `kimi-k2.6`: `0.5%`

Length normalization does **not** remove the gap.

Explicit endgame-goal references per `100` words:
- `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`: `0.634`
- `kimi-k2.6`: `0.266`
- `claude-opus-4-7`: `0.076`
- `gpt-5.1`: `0.017`

So this is not just “Gemini wrote more words.” Even after length normalization, Gemini references the actual objective far more often.

## Detailed Results

### Plan Length

Average words per plan:
- `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`: `175.9`
- `claude-opus-4-7`: `40.1`
- `gpt-5.1`: `24.5`
- `kimi-k2.6`: `8.5`

Interpretation:
- Gemini is much more verbose
- that is a real behavioral difference and a real deployment cost
- because of that, the length-normalized rates matter more than the raw plan-share counts

### Objective Tracking By Provider

| Provider | Explicit Endgame Goal | Quantified Goal | Aggressive Action | Contingency | Card Focus |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` | `58.5%` | `54.5%` | `81.4%` | `36.0%` | `49.0%` |

Interpretation:
- Gemini is the outlier on explicit objective tracking
- Claude, GPT, and Kimi spend much more of their visible plan budget on contingencies and local tactical heuristics
- Claude still looks strategically structured, but not in an explicitly endgame-targeted way

### Goal Tracking Scales With Proximity To Victory

Gemini’s explicit goal tracking rises sharply as it gets closer to the `28/42` target:

- early (`0-9` territories): `39.8%`
- midgame (`10-19` territories): `69.9%`
- late (`20+` territories): `100%`

Comparable rates for the others stay near zero:

`claude-opus-4-7`
- early: `2.6%`
- midgame: `2.7%`
- late: `33.3%` on only `3` late turns

`gpt-5.1`
- early: `0.0%`
- midgame: `0.9%`
- late: `0.0%`

`kimi-k2.6`
- early: `0.7%`
- midgame: `1.5%`
- late: `25.0%` on only `4` late turns

This is the strongest single planning-trace result in the analysis.

Gemini is not merely mentioning the win condition occasionally. It appears to update the explicit salience of the goal as the board state approaches a reachable finish.

## Outcome Association

Across all providers pooled:
- turns with explicit endgame-goal language averaged `5.189` territories gained
- turns without it averaged `4.080`

So explicit goal-tracking plans were associated with about `+1.11` additional territories on the same turn.

This is not causal proof, but it is directionally consistent with the hypothesis that objective-tracking language reflects a more conversion-oriented local strategy.

Within Gemini itself:
- explicit-goal turns averaged `5.206` territories gained
- non-explicit turns averaged `4.590`

Within Claude:
- explicit-goal turns averaged `5.000`
- non-explicit turns averaged `4.068`

The same direction appears, but Gemini produces far more of these turns than the others.

Interpretation:

- Gemini does it far more often, and much more continuously across board states

## What This Suggests

The planning traces support a specific mechanism for Gemini’s earlier full-stack win lead:

1. Gemini keeps the terminal objective live in the visible plan text.
2. Gemini quantifies the remaining gap much more often.
3. Gemini increases that explicit goal tracking as it gets closer to a winnable state.
4. That behavioral pattern is consistent with stronger agentic drive in a bounded live loop, where objective drift is costly.

That is a stronger and more interesting claim than simply saying Gemini “has better plans.”

## Caveats

1. This is a lexical coding analysis, not a direct window into hidden reasoning.
2. Gemini is much more verbose, so raw plan-share comparisons alone would overstate the difference; that is why length-normalized counts are included.
3. The outcome association is correlational, not causal.
4. The analysis is specific to this live Risk harness and this prompt pack.

## Bottom Line

The pooled `32`-game provider series supports the claim that Gemini is markedly more explicit about the actual win objective in its observable planning traces.

That difference is:
- large in raw plan-share terms
- still large after length normalization
- strongest when Gemini is close to victory

This is the current best mechanism-level evidence for the hypothesis that part of Gemini’s live-game edge comes from more persistent goal direction, not just generic tactical competence.
