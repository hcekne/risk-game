## Question

Gemini won the pooled provider championship `20 / 32`.

What execution pattern actually explains that edge?

The important point here is that “execution” means observable turn behavior:
- placement validity
- attack chaining
- fallback use
- timeout drag
- whether the model converts a live turn into actual territory gains

This note does **not** try to infer hidden search or hidden reasoning.

## Data And Method

Dataset:
- pooled provider series: `/shared-game-results/experiment_series/frontier_strategic_provider_32_pooled`
- `32` games
- `946` saved turn summaries

Method:
- use the saved `turn_summary_turn_*.json` traces only
- read:
  - `derived` turn metrics
  - `events`
  - `llm_decisions`
- restrict the phase analysis to non-planning steps:
  - `troop_placement`
  - `attack`
  - `fortify`
  - card-trade phases

Tracked execution features:
- turns with execution fallbacks
- turns with invalid actions
- attack-turn share
- zero-attack turn share
- long attack-chain share (`4+` wins, `6+` wins)
- large territory-swing share
- midgame conversion (`10-19` starting territories)
- phase-level fallback and invalid rates

## Headline Result

Gemini is **not** the cleanest runtime in the field.

Claude is cleaner.

Gemini wins because it combines:
- near-Claude attack legality
- much stronger high-conquest chaining
- much better midgame territory conversion
- very low execution idling

That combination matters more than raw cleanliness alone.

## What Actually Makes Gemini Better

The execution trace does **not** support a simplistic story like:
- Gemini wins because it is the cleanest
- Gemini wins because the others are broken

The better summary is:

1. Claude is cleaner, but less explosive.
2. GPT is aggressive enough, but too timeout-contaminated.
3. Kimi is cheap and sometimes sharp, but less continuous and less disciplined in later phases.
4. Gemini wins because it combines:
   - very low idle turns
   - the deepest conquest chains
   - the best midgame territory conversion
   - attack-phase legality strong enough not to waste that aggression

That is the observable execution mechanism behind the `20 / 32` Gemini provider lead.

## Caveats

1. This is still an observational trace analysis, not a causal intervention.
2. Some error patterns reflect the specific live timer policy and parser/output grammar, not abstract strategy alone.
3. The strongest claim here is about this harness, not about all possible agent setups.

## Bottom Line

Gemini’s provider-championship edge is best explained by **conversion quality**, not just by reliability.

Claude is cleaner.
GPT is often just as aggressive.
Kimi is sometimes efficient.

But Gemini is the model that most consistently turns a live turn into a long, high-yield conquest chain without bleeding enough execution quality to cancel out the gain.
